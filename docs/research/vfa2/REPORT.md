One-dimensional Gaussian kernel k(x,y)=exp(-(x-y)^2/(2 ell^2)). A section expansion f=sum a_i k(.,c_i) has inner product a^T K(c,d)b for expansions of the same width. Reproducing evaluation f(z)=<f,k(.,z)>; check analytic norms and parallelogram law. Sum and scalar scaling preserve finite mixtures; product exactly expands Gaussian terms with precision added and amplitude reduced by exp(-(a-b)^2/(2(s^2+t^2))). Same-width products can change width, mixed widths refuse the single-width RKHS norm. Do not pretend a fixed-kernel space is closed under arbitrary products or that all Gaussian mixtures have a proven norm in a single RKHS. Derivative evaluation and whole-line integration computed from analytic Gaussian identities, checked against finite differences and SciPy quadrature. Bounds: 256 terms, width [0.001,1000], bounded vectors; no silent truncation.

Kernel interpolation uses Cholesky and rejects duplicate sites and condition > 1e9. For fixed distinct sites X, P_X(z)^2 = k(z,z) - k_X(z)^T K_X^-1 k_X(z). The interpolant is the RKHS projection; |f(z)-s_X(z)| <= ||f||_H P_X(z). Test an explicit residual-section extremizer giving equality for a unit-norm witness; validate via independent linear solve and analytic target. Target norm must be supplied externally; samples do not certify it. Float64 power estimates and clamping near zero are not certified interval bounds.

Gaussian FPE: omega_j sampled IID N(0,ell^-2), coordinate feature exp(i omega_j x), similarity Re average exp(i omega_j(x-y)). Section expansion coefficient vector is sum a_i exp(i omega_j c_i); decode with conjugate coordinate feature. For M pre-fixed pairs and IID frequencies, Hoeffding/union bound yields epsilon=min(2,sqrt(2 log(2M/alpha)/D)) with failure probability <=alpha. For a fixed expansion and fixed queries, decoding error <=sum |a_i| epsilon under that event. Not a uniform continuum guarantee, not applicable to learned or arbitrary supplied frequencies; direct FPE constructor accepts explicit frequencies but theorem is conditional on IID sampling. No adaptive selection or statistical guarantee for arbitrary numeric input. Finite precision and phase errors unqualified. Explicit features, Gaussian section representation and prior Fourier periodic representation remain distinct.
